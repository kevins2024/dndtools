# Senkolai

> **Draft status:** this is a from-scratch continent proposal, built overnight
> for review — nothing here is canon yet, nothing is cross-linked into
> `world.json` or `src/data/`, and no existing lore references it. Read it,
> keep what's useful, discard or rewrite the rest with no ceremony required.
> Built deliberately _against_ the grain of both known continents: no
> lateral map-of-nations like [[Kaemahz]]'s seven regions, and no
> crater/interior-sea geology like [[Yetgrese]]'s. Different axis entirely —
> this one is organized by _height_, not by border.

One-line pitch: **a continent that is a single living thing** — one
impossibly old, impossibly large clonal tree-organism with five surviving
trunks, and a civilization that has spent longer building _inside_ it than
most nations spend existing. Culture here is stratified by altitude, not
geography. Your nation isn't which country you were born in — it's which
_band_ of the tree you were born on.

## Overview

Far south of Solvale with treacherous waters and shifting currents between and a vast distance means no one in Kaemahz is aware it exists.

There is no native word for the continent as a whole. None of the five
Boles consider themselves part of a shared landmass-identity — each
regards itself as the center, and the others as neighbors at best,
curiosities at worst. \*\*"Senkolai" will be its name for this document. Each of the five boles and each of the vertical levels have their own names.

## The Everbole

The whole concept: it's one organism. What looks, from a ship's deck, like
five separate sky-scratching trees — to be **one continuous root system**, feeding five
above-ground trunks (the Boles) that have grown independently for so long
they no longer resemble each other, share no aboveground connection, and
would be classified as different species entirely if anyone tried. They are
the same living thing. Insiders call it **the Everbole** (name pending), when they call it
anything — most people below Kelmoth just call it "the root," lowercase,
the way you'd refer to weather.

Its age is unknown and unknowable by any method locals have. Its lower
trunk sections — the mid-height mass that the Mistbelt and much of the
Root Deep are built in and on — are no longer properly _alive_ in the way
the crown is: they petrified into a stone-wood hybrid material over some
unfathomable span, dense and workable like fine-grained granite but grained
like heartwood, called **stonewood** (name pending) locally. The crown canopy at the top
is still fully, riotously alive. The deep root tangle at the very bottom is
alive too, and still slowly growing, still slowly searching — for what, if
anything, nobody who lives there claims to know, though the deepest root
miners have opinions they don't share with outsiders. The mines are accessible through shaft 'dug' in the petrified wood near the water and must remain in the interior of the trees protection until reaching stone below surface. Any miscalculated strike results in a flood of seawater that must be dangerously repaired or the shaft abandoned.

This means the continent's "geology" is actually anatomy. What Kaemahz would
call a mountain range is the Everbole's fossilized trunk-mass. What would be
called a cave system is stonewood heartwood, hollowed by insects, water,
and centuries of deliberate excavation. There is no volcanism here, no
tectonic drama — the closest thing to a natural disaster is a **limb-fall** (or an ocean storm),
when an aging crown branch the size of a small kingdom's forest finally lets
go and comes down through the lower bands, which is exactly as catastrophic
and as survivable-with-warning as it sounds, and which every Bole has a
different name for and a different set of rituals around.

## Geography: three bands, five Boles

Every one of the five Boles is internally divided into the same three
vertical bands — this is the real cultural map of Senkolai, cutting across
Bole identity the way a social class might cut across a Kaemahz kingdom.
A Canopy-dweller from Sennar and a Canopy-dweller from Kelmoth have more in
common with each other, culturally, than either does with the Root Deep two
miles straight down in their own Bole. This shared cultural divide hints at a more unified people in ages past.

**The Reach** (bad name, needs changed) (canopy, top band) — the living crown, kilometers of interwoven
branch-mass thick enough to build on, with settlements strung across
branch-forks and grown-lattice platforms rather than cleared ground. Full
sun, constant wind, near-constant vertigo for anyone not raised in it.
Settlements are light, rope-and-platform construction that flexes with the
wind rather than fighting it — a Reach building that doesn't sway in a storm
is considered badly built, not well built. Population here skews toward
folk with some means of not falling: aarakocra communities are the oldest
established presence, alongside tabaxi who favor the branch-running life,
and a long-adapted human lineage — Reach-born humans, no different in stock (mmm, maybe no humans here)
from anyone in Kaemahz but raised from infancy on platforms with gaps, and
famous (or notorious, depending who's asked) among lowlanders for an
essentially nonexistent fear of heights and a corresponding total inability
to sleep well indoors on solid ground.

**The Shroud** (mistbelt, middle band) — a near-permanent fog layer where the
living crown gives way to the petrified stonewood trunk-mass beneath it,
thick enough that direct sunlight is a rumor and most navigation is done by
sound, smell, and the bioluminescent fungal colonies that carpet every
horizontal stonewood surface. Culturally the most inward-facing of the
three bands — the fog discourages both casual travel and casual conquest,
and Shroud settlements have historically just... not needed doors that lock,
because nobody comes through who wasn't already meant to be there. Firbolgs
are the dominant folk here, alongside gnomes who took to fungal cultivation
and bioluminescent artifice with real enthusiasm, and a small, quiet human
lineage that's adapted toward genuinely excellent low-light vision over
generations rather than any magical means anyone can point to.

**The Root Deep** (bottom band) — the excavated stonewood heartwood and the
true earth beneath it, where the trunk gives way to the still-living, still-
searching root tangle. Warmer than the bands above (something about the
Everbole's own slow metabolism), tunnel-built rather than platform-built,
and the most socially rigid of the three bands — lineage matters enormously
here, in a way it doesn't above, because a family's claim to a particular
seam of stonewood or root-cavity is inherited property, defended for
generations. Goliaths adapted to tunnel life rather than open sky are the
oldest confirmed presence, working alongside deep gnomes and tortles, whose
shells and temperament suit slow, patient stonewood excavation unusually
well. Root Deep folk regard both bands above them with something between
pity and contempt — "unrooted," in the local idiom, is not a compliment.

### The Five Boles

- **Kelmoth** — the oldest and largest trunk, and by informal but nearly
  universal consent, the _center_: not a capital (nothing here has a
  capital in the Kaemahz sense), but the site of the Everbole's oldest
  confirmed growth and the place every band's religious tradition agrees is
  spiritually significant, even though they disagree about almost
  everything else. Home to the **Concord of the Root** — not a government,
  a gathering, held on no fixed schedule (the last one was eleven years
  ago; the one before that, thirty-four) at a specific ring-platform where
  all three bands physically meet, called only when a dispute or a
  discovery is judged too large for one Bole to sit on alone. Whichever
  band hosts the current Concord provides the location; the ring-platform
  itself belongs to none of them.
- **Sennar** — the Reach at its most developed: the largest sky-trade hub
  on the continent, the closest thing Senkolai has to a "capital of the
  air," and the Bole most likely to have heard of Kaemahz at all, thanks to
  a trickle of glider-borne trade with Halcoro. Loud, confident, and
  unbothered by height in a way that unsettles every visitor from solid
  ground.
- **Dolkren** — the Root Deep's stronghold, the most insular and lineage-
  bound of the five, and the source of nearly all worked stonewood that
  ever leaves the continent. Outsiders are tolerated here for exactly as
  long as a trade transaction requires and not one hour longer. Dolkren
  miners are the only people on Senkolai who have ever, on record, broken
  through into open space _beneath_ the deepest confirmed root-mass — a
  handful of times, always sealed back up immediately, always described
  afterward in the same word across unrelated mining families who'd never
  spoken to each other: _wrong_.
- **Ozhmerel** — the Shroud's deepest expression, wrapped almost entirely
  in permanent fog even by mistbelt standards, and home to the **Listening
  Silence**, a religious-scholarly order that spends careers learning to
  hear the Everbole's own sap-pulse and treats what it hears as scripture
  in progress rather than fixed doctrine. Quietest place on the continent,
  by design and by culture, not by lack of population.
- **Halcoro** — the youngest Bole (a relative term — still centuries old),
  the least internally consistent, and the only real point of contact with
  the outside world. Its Root Deep meets tidal river-delta where the
  Everbole's roots finally reach salt water, in a brackish mangrove sprawl
  called **the Rootmouth** — the one place on Senkolai with a functioning,
  Kaemahz-recognizable _port_. Humans are the plurality here, not because
  they dominate anywhere else on the continent but because Halcoro is where
  outsiders, refugees, and anyone who didn't fit cleanly into a Reach,
  Shroud, or Root Deep identity elsewhere have been drifting for
  generations. If Senkolai ever produces a diplomat, a translator, or a
  ship Kaemahz has heard of, they're from Halcoro.

### The Windscar

Past the edge of the Everbole's root-reach, where the ground stops being
part of the organism at all, the land breaks into a wind-scoured plain the
locals call **the Windscar** — the one part of Senkolai that would look
almost familiar to a Kaemahz traveler, except for the wind, which does not
stop, ever, and shapes everything from the architecture (there isn't much)
to the culture (thoroughly nomadic) to the local wildlife (large, low, and
built to not get picked up). The **Windscar clans** ride tamed windhawks —
condor-sized, sail-winged, genuinely dangerous animals that a rider spends
a decade earning the trust of — and treat the Everbole's people as strange,
sedentary, slightly pitiable cousins, which is an opinion returned with
equal enthusiasm in the other direction. Genasi (air-touched, mostly) and
hardy human lineages make up the clans in roughly even measure; nobody
keeps a census, since a census requires people to stay in one place long
enough to be counted.

## Demographics

No single species or lineage dominates Senkolai the way humans anchor
Kaemahz or dragonborn concentrate in its south. Roughly: aarakocra and
Reach-adapted humans in the canopy; firbolgs, gnomes, and low-light-adapted
humans in the mistbelt; goliaths, deep gnomes, and tortles in the root
deep; air genasi and windrider humans on the Windscar; and everyone, in
whatever proportion currently holds, in Halcoro. Dwarves and dragonborn
exist here but only as rare individual travelers or transplants — neither
has an established community, and that's worth preserving rather than
"fixing," since both already anchor other parts of the setting.

## Religion and the three afterlives

There is no pantheon here, personified or otherwise — nothing like
Tellonde's Greek-with-twists gods, and nothing like Fol's spirit-shamanism
either, though it shares Fol's instinct that the divine is a present,
physical thing rather than a distant one. What's venerated is the Everbole
itself, and — this is the load-bearing detail — **the three bands disagree,
sincerely and without much rancor, about what happens to a person when they
die**, and the disagreement maps exactly onto the vertical geography:

- **The Reach** holds that a spirit _rises_ — that dying properly means
  ascending past the crown entirely, and that Reach funeral rites (a body
  released from the highest platform available, weighted with nothing) are
  designed to give the spirit the cleanest possible path upward.
- **The Root Deep** holds the mirror belief: that a spirit _descends_,
  returning to and feeding the root tangle that fed it in life, which is
  why root-deep burial is literal — a body worked back into a claimed
  root-cavity, becoming part of the family's inherited ground.
- **The Shroud**, in between both geographically and theologically, holds
  that a spirit does neither — that it _lingers_, becoming part of the fog
  itself, neither rising nor descending, which is the belief the Listening
  Silence in Ozhmerel has built an entire scholarly tradition around:
  if the dead linger in the mist, then the mist can, in principle, be
  listened to.

None of the three bands considers the others heretical, exactly — more
like each thinks the others are describing a different, smaller truth. A
Reach funeral held for a Root Deep-born spouse is a genuine diplomatic
occasion, handled the way an interfaith wedding might be handled elsewhere.

## Magic, nature, and the sap-tide

Primal and druidic traditions are the load-bearing magic of Senkolai in a
way arcane wizardry simply isn't — a traveling Kaemahz wizard is treated
with the mild, polite bewilderment of someone practicing a craft everyone
agrees is clever but nobody quite sees the point of, when the Everbole
itself is right there to work with instead. Druids, primal casters, and
the Listening Silence's sap-readers hold the cultural position clerics and
wizards split between them in Kaemahz.

Layered on top of Tellonde's base calendar (the same 204-day year, four
51-day seasons, that governs the rest of the known world) is a purely
local rhythm: the **sap-tide**, a roughly 17-day cycle — about three per
season, a little over a dozen a year — during which the Everbole's sap
visibly rises through the whole structure. During a **Tide**, the crown
flushes with new color, Shroud bioluminescence intensifies until the fog
itself seems to glow, root-deep tunnels warm perceptibly, and climbing or
traveling _with_ the grain of the trunk gets measurably easier — several
druidic and primal effects are locally held (and, per the Listening
Silence's records, reliably observed) to work better during a Tide.
Between Tides is **the Still** — drier, quieter, harder climbing, but the
best working conditions for Dolkren's stonewood miners, since the receded
sap exposes seams a Tide would hide. Any Senkolai native can tell you
without thinking which phase the continent is currently in; it's as
unremarkable and as load-bearing to them as knowing the time of day.

## Trade and export

Senkolai's exports are valuable specifically because nothing else produces
them: **stonewood** (Dolkren's worked stone-grain heartwood, strong and
fine enough for tool and weapon hafts that never warp); **heartglass** (rare
naturally-formed clear amber-like petrified sap, prized by artificers and
jewelers for lenses and inlay, found only in old stonewood seams and
already commanding absurd prices in the one or two Kaemahz ports that have
seen a sample); bioluminescent fungal cultures from the Shroud, which
survive transplant surprisingly well and are quietly becoming a novelty
lighting trade in coastal Kaemahz cities; and windhawk feathers from the
Windscar clans, who trade them reluctantly and only through Halcoro
intermediaries, since parting with a feather from a living, bonded bird is
a genuine cultural taboo and most trade feathers come from natural molt or
a bird's death of old age.

## Naming conventions

Kept distinct per band deliberately, since the bands don't consider
themselves one culture:

- **Reach names**: flowing, vowel-heavy, often built from compounded
  wind/light/height concepts, frequently using an apostrophe to mark a
  glottal stop between vowel clusters. Soft consonants only (l, n, m, r,
  s, y, h) — no hard stops. _Examples: Sennar, Aelu'nar, Yothrelin, Sai'omeh._
- **Shroud names**: soft, sibilant, whisper-register — sh/zh, f, s, th
  sounds dominate, words tend short. _Examples: Ozhmerel, Shuvenn, Fenzashi._
- **Root Deep names**: short, hard, percussive, built on consonant
  clusters (kr, dr, gn, str) and closed syllables — the opposite instinct
  from Reach naming entirely. _Examples: Dolkren, Krast, Gundrek, Ovrik._
- **Windscar names**: clan-based rather than place-based, since there are
  no fixed settlements to name — a clan is named for the storm it was
  founded under or the windhawk lineage it rides, in blunt descriptive
  compounds. _Examples: Redgale Clan, the Duskhowl Riders, Longwing Clan._
- **Halcoro names**: deliberately inconsistent — a trade-pidgin blend of
  all three band-languages plus borrowed Kaemahz fragments. The
  inconsistency _is_ the convention; a "proper" Halcoro name is one that
  sounds like it came from nowhere in particular.

Avoid: any -march/-wald/-vath/-vale/-dal suffix pattern (heavily used
across Kaemahz already), a Tor- prefix (flagged overused there too), and
V-heavy names generally (Fol's tribes already lean hard on that sound).

## Mysteries and DM hooks

- **What's beneath the roots.** Dolkren's rare breakthroughs into open
  space under the deepest confirmed root-mass are real, on record, and
  unexplained. "Wrong" is the only word that survives every independent
  account. Nobody has stayed down there long enough to describe it further.
  Left deliberately open, same spirit as Yetgrese's crater origin and
  Kaemahz's Orvath teleport circle — a hook, not an answer.
- **Whether the Everbole is aware.** No consensus, even among the
  Listening Silence, on whether the organism they've built a religion
  around has anything like a mind, or whether the sap-pulse is just
  biology being mistaken for meaning. A visiting skeptic and a lifelong
  believer would have a genuinely unresolved argument, not a "the DM knows
  and one side is just wrong" argument.
- **The next limb-fall.** Every Bole tracks warning signs (canopy stress,
  sap-tide irregularities) differently, and a limb-fall large enough to
  hit more than one Bole at once has apparently never happened in living
  memory — which either means it can't, or means nobody's been watching
  long enough yet.
- **First real contact.** Nothing here assumes Senkolai has ever sent
  anyone to Kaemahz, or vice versa, successfully and repeatably — the
  Halcoro trade trickle is thin and one-directional so far. A party being
  the first documented round trip is entirely on the table.
